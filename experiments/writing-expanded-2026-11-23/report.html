What changed

Related changes in one grammar structure now remain one correction. Previously, a correct model rewrite such as “I wonder where she is” could be split into two broken cards. Auxiliary changes and dependent verb forms, embedded questions and short reorderings are grouped; unrelated errors remain separate.

Up to 30 proposed corrections are reviewed in batches of six under the original total timeout, replacing the ten-finding rejection. The twelve-error passage now passes on every model. A failed later batch rejects the analysis rather than exposing unchecked partial feedback. Explicit code/literal labels are protected. Ordinary vocabulary highlights in backticks remain correctable and retain their Markdown markers.

Cases and method

The main set has 96 passages: 40 grammar, 10 structural, 30 correct-text controls, 12 multi-error paragraphs, two long contexts (about 1,800 and 4,200 characters), a repeated-target case and a twelve-error capacity case. One ambiguous “recommend me a book” construction is diagnostic only, leaving 95 scored passages per model.

The independent follow-up has 16 new contrastive passages plus the 24 previous basic regressions per model. Three additional marked-text passages test inflection, capitalization and American spelling inside backticks. In total, the final scored sets contain 552 model–passage results, of which 540 pass. The original basics pass 96/96. A further 64 correction/gap exercises were generated and validated against their approved corrections.

Baseline inference was live for all 384 main analyses. Later verification reuses responses only when the entire serialized request is identical, including model tag, system prompt, source input, schema and generation parameters. Changed requests go to Ollama. The first 160 holdout analyses were live; final verification reuses identical requests. Final main and holdout postprocessing was additionally replayed with 998 exact request-payload matches. Cache/live counts are in the summary and each trace preserves provenance. Reuse is not an independent repeated inference test; no cached timings are used to claim faster generation.

Gold corrections are scorer-only and never enter model prompts. A corpus-construction typo and accepted alternative tense/inversion readings are documented in gold errata and applied equally to baseline and candidate. A passing case requires every expected local correction, no extra edit and no failure. Different target anchors can pass if their one-edit source sentence is identical.

Remaining errors

Gemma sometimes overcorrects plural agreement after none. Qwen sometimes changes a factual past conditional from was to were, or changes a correct statement about a British spelling. Gemma 3 misses one collocation in a multi-error paragraph. These failures are retained in the results, rather than excluded or repaired by hard-coded expected answers.

Limits and feedback quality

Exact-edit correctness does not certify the explanation. In the holdout, Gemma 3 correctly changes “None of the equipment are working” to “is working”, but its explanation calls equipment plural and overgeneralizes the rule for none. Explanations were spot-checked, not exhaustively graded. Cards still need user review. These tests establish regression behavior for the selected passages; they do not prove stability or correctness for every book, learner text or model sampling run.
